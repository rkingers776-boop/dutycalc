US Import Duty on UK Goods — The Transatlantic Trade Reality

The US and UK run roughly $300 billion annually in bilateral trade. UK goods enter under MFN rates averaging 3.5% across Scotch whisky, automotive parts, luxury apparel (Savile Row tailoring, Scottish cashmere), pharmaceuticals, books and publishing, and aerospace components. There is no US-UK free trade agreement, so those rates are the general schedule — and since 24 July 2026 a 10% forced-labour layer sits on top of them for UK-origin goods.

Real numbers: $3,000 of Scotch whisky + $200 shipping = $3,200 CIF. Duty at 3.5% (where applicable — spirits rates vary by proof liter) = $112. MPF = $11.09. HMF = $4. Total: $127.09 on $3,200 — effective 4.0%. But spirits also face TTB federal excise tax, a separate and often much larger cost. Customs duty is frequently the smallest line item on a Scotch whisky entry summary; the federal excise dwarfs it.

The $800 De Minimis — Suspended Since August 2025

Section 321 used to exempt shipments under $800 from all duties, taxes and fees. It stopped doing that on 29 August 2025, for every country of origin. A £600 Barbour jacket, £450 John Lobb shoes, a £200 bottle of single malt — all now dutiable from the first dollar, on top of the 10% forced-labour layer that took effect on 24 July 2026. What has not changed is US sales tax: it remains a state-level charge collected at point of sale, so the import itself creates no sales-tax event. Set that against UK-to-EU trade after Brexit, where the £135 threshold, 20% VAT and full customs declarations still apply — the US is still the simpler destination for UK DTC e-commerce, just no longer a free one. Category-Specific Duty Breakdown for Major UK Exports

  • Scotch whisky (2208.30): Specific rate ~0–2.8¢/proof liter. PLUS TTB federal excise at $13.50/proof gallon. The excise dwarfs customs. Case of 12 bottles at 40% ABV: customs ~$1.08, excise ~$27. Customs is the rounding error; excise is the real cost.
  • Automotive parts (8708): 2.5% average. UK suppliers — GKN, Delphi, Johnson Matthey — ship billions in parts to US assembly plants and aftermarket. One of the largest UK-to-US export categories.
  • Apparel (Chapters 61–62): 2.5–32% depending on fabric and garment type. Scottish cashmere sweaters at 4–6%, Savile Row wool suits at 17.5%. The HTS subheading matters enormously.
  • Books (Chapter 49): 0% MFN. Books carry no MFN duty into the US. Since 24 July 2026 the 10% forced-labour layer is the only duty element on them — a real change for UK publishers such as Penguin Random House and the Oxford and Cambridge university presses, which move millions of units annually.
  • Aerospace parts (Chapter 88): 0–3%. Rolls-Royce engines, BAE Systems components. Low rates reflect deeply integrated US-UK aerospace supply chains.
  • Pharmaceuticals (Chapter 30): Mostly 0% MFN. GSK and AstraZeneca account for tens of billions in annual UK-to-US pharma trade. The MFN line is zero, and the 10% forced-labour layer is the only duty element — still the lightest tariff treatment of any UK export sector.

Proposed US-UK Trade Agreement — Still Not Here

The US and UK have been discussing a comprehensive FTA since Brexit. Multiple rounds, nothing ratified as of 2026. A pharmaceutical GMP mutual recognition agreement has been signed — one of the few sectoral deals. Tariffs remain at MFN levels for the vast majority of goods. If a full FTA eventually passes, a large share of UK-US trade would drop to 0% — particularly benefiting automotive, machinery, and certain agriculture. Do not budget on it happening soon. US trade negotiations move slowly and the UK is not the highest-priority bilateral partner for the USTR. For comparison: EU to US duty guide.

FDA and TTB Compliance for UK Food, Beverage, and Spirits

For spirits: importers need a TTB Federal Basic Permit and COLA label approval before CBP clears the shipment. Scotch whisky must meet the US standard of identity — produced in Scotland, aged at least 3 years in oak casks, minimum 40% ABV. Single malt, blended, and grain Scotch each have distinct TTB labeling rules. For food products: FDA facility registration, prior notice filing, and full labeling compliance are mandatory. UK food labeling (traffic-light system, dual metric/imperial) is generally compatible with US requirements, but the US nutrition facts panel format is mandatory and does not accept the UK equivalent. UK exporters must produce a US-compliant label — missing FDA prior notice results in detention.

Shipping UK to US — Post-Brexit Logistics

UK goods ship directly with a UK certificate of origin — no intermediate EU customs territory. Key UK departure ports: Felixstowe, Southampton, London Gateway (ocean); Heathrow (air). US entry ports: New York/Newark, Savannah, Houston. Southampton to NY: 7–9 days — one of the fastest transatlantic ocean routes. LHR to JFK: overnight. UK luxury and high-value e-commerce is overwhelmingly air-freighted via integrators (DHL, FedEx, UPS). With de minimis suspended, even single-parcel consumer orders now carry duty, so the choice of integrator — and the entry type it files — matters more than it used to.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the duty rate for UK goods entering the US?

The average is 3.5% MFN, but it varies widely by product category, and since 24 July 2026 the effective rate is roughly ten points higher. Most manufactured goods run 0–5% MFN. Books enter at 0%. Scotch whisky carries a specific rate of approximately 0–2.8¢ per proof liter (plus TTB federal excise). Automotive parts average 2.5%. Apparel ranges from 2.5% to 32% depending on fabric and garment type. Add the 2026 Section 301 forced-labour layer, which is additive for UK-origin goods rather than a floor: it is charged on top of the MFN rate, taking the average from 3.5% to about 13.5%.

Is there a US-UK free trade agreement?

No comprehensive FTA exists. Negotiations have been ongoing since Brexit, but nothing has been ratified. A handful of limited sectoral agreements — most notably a pharmaceutical GMP mutual recognition agreement — are in force. UK goods pay standard MFN rates. Do not budget on a full FTA passing soon; US trade negotiations move slowly and the UK is not the USTR's highest-priority bilateral partner.

Do UK shipments under $800 enter duty-free now that Section 321 is suspended?

No. The Section 321 exemption has been suspended since 29 August 2025 for every country of origin, so a UK parcel under $800 is dutiable from the first dollar. A £600 Barbour jacket, £450 John Lobb shoes, or a £200 bottle of single malt now pays the 3.5% MFN rate plus the 10% forced-labour layer in force since 24 July 2026. The $800 line survives only as history — the threshold Congress raised in 2016 and that executive action suspended nine years later. See the de minimis value guide for the current position.

Does post-Brexit affect UK-to-US duty rates?

No. UK-to-US trade was always outside the EU customs union for US tariff purposes. The US has always treated the UK as a separate country of origin. Brexit changed nothing about how CBP classifies UK goods. Brexit transformed UK-to-EU trade, adding customs declarations, VAT, and rules-of-origin requirements — but UK-to-US trade was never inside that system. The transatlantic corridor is unchanged.

ITA zero rates may apply
If your UK export falls under the WTO Information Technology Agreement, it enters the US at a zero MFN rate even without an FTA — but the 10% forced-labour layer still applies on top. Check your HS code against the ITA product list. HS code lookup →
