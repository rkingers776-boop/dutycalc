German Import Duty on Chinese General Cargo

The EU TARIC (Tarif Intégré de l’Union Européenne) applies uniformly across Germany — there is no separate German tariff schedule for non-EU imports. For general cargo from China — consumer electronics, household goods, tools, and industrial components — the average duty rate sits at 4.2%. The German Zoll (customs administration) processes more Chinese imports than any other EU member state, handling millions of declarations annually through its ATLAS system.

Here is what the math looks like on a representative shipment: €1,200 in goods plus €80 in shipping and insurance gives a CIF value of €1,280. Customs duty at 4.2% on the CIF value equals €53.76. Then Einfuhrumsatzsteuer at 19% is applied to the CIF-plus-duty subtotal of €1,333.76, yielding €253.41 in VAT. Total import taxes come to €307.17, and the fully landed cost is €1,587.17. That is real money, and it is why getting the estimate right before you commit to a supplier matters.

Einfuhrumsatzsteuer at 19% — German Import VAT

Germany applies the standard 19% VAT rate on imports, formally called Einfuhrumsatzsteuer (EUSt). A reduced rate of 7% applies to a narrower set of goods: books, certain foodstuffs, some medical supplies, and a handful of cultural items. The vast majority of Chinese general cargo — electronics, tools, household items — attracts the full 19%.

EUSt is calculated on a base of CIF value plus customs duty plus any applicable excise taxes. For VAT-registered German businesses, import VAT is fully recoverable through the monthly Umsatzsteuer-Voranmeldung (UStVA) filed with the Finanzamt. You pay the EUSt at the time of customs clearance, report it as input tax in Box 61 of your UStVA, and deduct it in the same period. The net effect is a cash-flow impact — you finance the VAT for a few weeks — not a permanent cost. Non-registered buyers and consumers bear the VAT as a final cost.

The €22/€150 Split — Germany’s De Minimis Reality

Before July 2021, goods with a value under €22 were exempt from both customs duty and VAT. That regime is gone. Under the current rules that Germany enforces as part of the EU Customs Code:

  • Customs duty de minimis: No duty is charged on shipments where the goods value (excluding shipping) is under €150. Above €150, the applicable TARIC rate kicks in on the full CIF value.
  • VAT: No de minimis. Einfuhrumsatzsteuer applies from €1 upward, collected either through IOSS at the point of sale or through the carrier and customs at the border.

Germany was one of the heaviest users of the old €22 exemption because of its high cross-border e-commerce volume from China. The shift to universal VAT collection has been particularly impactful for German DTC brands that relied on sub-€22 shipments to keep prices low. Read more in our de minimis value guide.

IOSS — How German Sellers Handle Sub-€150 Chinese Imports

The Import One-Stop Shop (IOSS) is the EU’s mechanism for simplifying VAT on B2C consignments with an intrinsic value under €150. A seller registers for IOSS in one EU member state — Germany works, and registration goes through the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — and then charges German VAT (19% or 7%, depending on the goods) at the point of sale. The seller remits VAT monthly through the IOSS portal. The goods clear customs at the German border without any VAT stop; the customer receives the package with no surprise charges or carrier handling fees.

One important caveat: IOSS is designed for B2C distance sales. It does not apply to B2B stock movements. If you are an Amazon FBA seller sending goods from China to German fulfillment centers, those shipments enter under standard import procedures with a full customs declaration. The IOSS exemption does not cover inventory restocking. See also our calculators for China to France and China to Netherlands hub if you are routing through other EU entry points.

German Customs Clearance — The ATLAS System

Germany runs customs clearance through ATLAS (Automatisiertes Tarif- und Lokales Zoll-Abwicklungssystem), one of the most sophisticated electronic customs platforms in the EU. Clearance moves through major entry points: Hamburg for sea freight — Europe’s third-largest container port by volume — Frankfurt Airport for air freight, and Bremerhaven as a secondary sea gateway. You need an EORI number (Economic Operators Registration and Identification) to file declarations. EORI registration is free through the German Zoll-Portal and is typically issued within days.

Standard clearance runs 1–2 days for sea freight and often just hours for express air freight at Frankfurt. That said, the Zoll does conduct physical inspections more frequently than some other EU customs authorities, particularly on textiles and consumer electronics from China. If your shipment gets flagged for a Warenbeschau (physical examination), add a day or two and a handling fee from your freight forwarder. Keep your paperwork clean and your commodity codes precise.

Key German TARIC Classifications for Chinese Goods

Knowing the right tariff chapter before you ship avoids surprises and potential Nacherhebungen (retroactive duty demands). Here are the major chapters that cover Chinese general cargo entering Germany:

  • Chapter 85: Electrical machinery and equipment — 0% to 4.2%. ITA-covered electronics at 0%, consumer electronics at the upper end.
  • Chapter 84: Mechanical appliances and machinery — 0% to 2.7%. Industrial equipment, pumps, and machine parts fall here.
  • Chapters 61–62: Apparel and clothing accessories — 5% to 12%. Germany is Europe’s largest apparel import market, and these rates bite.
  • Chapter 94: Furniture, bedding, and prefabricated buildings — 0% to 5.6%.
  • Chapter 73: Articles of iron or steel — 0% to 3.7%. Fasteners, fittings, and structural components.

German customs is strict on correct classification. The Hauptzollamt can and does issue Nacherhebungen — retroactive duty bills — for misclassified goods going back up to 3 years. If customs alleges intent or fraud (Steuerhinterziehung), the look-back period extends to 10 years. Classify correctly the first time.

German Product Safety and Compliance

Germany’s Marktüberwachung (market surveillance) regime is among the strictest in the EU. For Chinese goods entering the German market, compliance is not optional:

  • CE Marking: Required for all products covered by EU harmonisation legislation. You need an EU Declaration of Conformity and technical documentation on file.
  • ElektroG (Electrical and Electronic Equipment Act): If you place electronics on the German market, you must register with the Stiftung EAR (Stiftung Elektro-Altgeräte Register) before the first sale. Registration is mandatory and failure to register carries fines up to €200,000.
  • VerpackG (Packaging Act): Anyone shipping packaged goods into Germany commercially must register with the Zentrale Stelle Verpackungsregister (ZSVR) and license their packaging with a dual system. This applies even if you are a non-German company shipping directly to German consumers.

These requirements are separate from customs clearance — you can clear customs and still be in violation of ElektroG or VerpackG. Competitor Abmahnungen (cease-and-desist warnings) are a real risk in Germany; non-compliance is enforced both by authorities and by competitors with standing to sue. Frequently Asked Questions

What’s the duty rate from China to Germany?

The average TARIC rate for general cargo from China is 4.2%. Information Technology Agreement (ITA) electronics enter at 0%. Textiles and apparel range from 5% to 12% depending on the specific commodity code. The 4.2% figure is a safe estimate for electronics, household goods, tools, and general merchandise. Always verify your specific HS code in the TARIC database before shipping — a 4-digit chapter guess is not enough for a binding estimate.

Do I pay VAT on Chinese imports under €22?

Since July 2021, yes — the €22 VAT exemption no longer exists. Einfuhrumsatzsteuer at 19% (or 7% for reduced-rate goods) applies from the first euro of commercial import value. The only surviving de minimis threshold is for customs duty: no duty is charged when the goods value (excluding shipping) is under €150. VAT has zero de minimis floor. If you sell to German consumers, collect VAT at checkout via IOSS or expect the carrier to collect it at the door with a handling surcharge.

How does IOSS work for German customers?

For B2C shipments with an intrinsic value under €150, you register for IOSS in any EU member state. At checkout, you charge German VAT (19% standard or 7% reduced) based on the customer’s delivery address. You remit the collected VAT monthly through the IOSS portal. The goods clear customs at the German border without a VAT stop — the customs declaration references your IOSS number and the shipment is released. The customer receives a clean delivery with no surprise charges, no carrier handling fee, and no delay at the border. For the customer experience alone, IOSS is worth setting up if you do any meaningful volume of B2C sales into Germany.

Do I need special registrations to sell Chinese electronics in Germany?

Yes — and they are separate from customs requirements. ElektroG registration with the Stiftung EAR is mandatory before placing any electronic or electrical equipment on the German market. VerpackG registration with the ZSVR is mandatory if you ship packaged goods into Germany commercially. You also need CE marking and an EU Declaration of Conformity for applicable products. These obligations apply regardless of whether you are based in Germany, elsewhere in the EU, or outside the EU entirely. Non-compliance can trigger Abmahnungen from competitors and fines from the authorities. None of these registrations are checked at customs clearance — customs releases your goods, and market surveillance catches up with you afterward.
