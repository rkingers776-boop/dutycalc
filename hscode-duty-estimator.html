Global HS Code Duty Estimator Guide

How the Harmonized System (HS) framework sets tariff rates worldwide — and how to estimate what you'll actually pay

What Is the HS Code Duty Estimator?

The Harmonized System (HS) is the universal language of international trade — a 6-digit commodity classification used by more than 200 countries. Every product that crosses a border gets an HS code, and that code determines the tariff rate. Our Global HS Code Duty Estimator uses a standardized baseline of 5.5% duty and 15% VAT to give you a realistic, mid-range estimate that applies to most generic tariff lines across major economies.

How the HS Code System Works

The HS code structure is hierarchical: the first 2 digits identify the chapter (broad product category), the next 2 narrow it to a heading, and the final 2 pin it to a subheading. For example, Chapter 61 covers knitted apparel, while Chapter 85 covers electrical machinery. Each country then adds its own digits beyond the 6-digit base to create 8-, 10-, or even 12-digit national tariff lines with specific duty rates.

Why a 5.5% Baseline?

Across the World Trade Organization's 166 member countries, the average Most-Favored-Nation (MFN) applied tariff on non-agricultural goods hovers around 5%–6%. We use 5.5% as a conservative baseline that reflects what you'd encounter on generic manufactured goods — electronics, tools, plastics, and general industrial components — entering most developed markets. It won't match every commodity (textiles and agriculture often run higher; IT goods under the ITA run at zero), but it's the right starting point for a first-pass landed cost check.

The HS code determines your base MFN rate. But for Chinese goods, Section 301 adds another layer on top — and that layer depends on which USTR list your product falls on, not your HS code. Our Section 301 China Tariffs guide explains the list system and how it stacks on whatever MFN rate your HS code gives you.

The 15% VAT Component

A 15% value-added tax is a common mid-point across global VAT and GST regimes. The EU averages around 21%, the UK sits at 20%, Australia and New Zealand run 10%–15% GST, and many developing economies apply 12%–18%. Our 15% figure gives a balanced estimate that's close enough for most non-EU, non-US corridors. Remember: VAT is calculated on the CIF value plus any customs duty already applied — so the effective tax base is always larger than the invoice price alone.

De Minimis Threshold — USD $50

Our estimator uses a USD $50 de minimis threshold, which reflects the lower end of global duty-free allowances. While the US offers a generous $800 Section 321 exemption, many countries are far stricter: the EU has a €22–€150 range depending on the tax type, Canada sits at CAD $20 for duty (though higher for GST), and some Southeast Asian nations have no de minimis at all. A $50 threshold means most commercial shipments will trigger duties — which is the safer assumption when you're planning.

The duty rate from your HS code only matters once you're above the de minimis threshold. Our De Minimis Value Guide shows the thresholds for every major destination — if your shipment value stays under the threshold, the HS code rate is academic.

How to Use This Estimator

Enter the commercial value of your goods (what you paid the supplier or the arms-length transaction price) and your shipping and insurance costs. The tool combines them into the CIF (Cost + Insurance + Freight) basis that customs uses. If your goods value exceeds the $50 threshold, the tool applies 5.5% duty to the full CIF amount, then 15% VAT on top of that duty-paid total. If you're under $50, both duty and VAT are zero. This is the standard customs math used by virtually every revenue authority on the planet.

When to Look Up the Exact HS Code

This estimator gives you a fast, directional number — perfect for comparing trade lanes or doing a quick sanity check before you commit to a supplier. But once you're past the quoting stage and ready to ship, you need the exact 10-digit HTS (US) or 8-digit TARIC (EU) code for your product. One digit wrong and your duty rate could swing by 5, 10, or even 20 percentage points. Use your country's official tariff schedule — the USITC HTS search, the EU TARIC database, or your local customs authority's online tool — to confirm the binding rate before you file entry paperwork.

What if my product's exact HS code gives a different rate than 5.5%?

The 5.5% is a baseline for quick estimates. If you have the exact 10-digit HTS code, use one of our country-specific calculators instead — they let you plug in the real rate.
