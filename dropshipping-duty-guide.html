Dropshipping Duty: Who Pays When the Parcel Arrives

Your first dropshipped order to the UK arrives, and the buyer refuses it — the courier is asking for £23 in VAT and clearance fees, and the customer didn't sign up for that. You eat the parcel, the freight, and the lesson. Every dropshipped order is an import, and someone owes something at the border. The question that decides whether your model works is who, and the answer is different in every country. This page maps it.

Thresholds per CBP / HMRC / EU rules in force · August 2026

The two-minute version
US: under $800, nothing — the friendliest market on earth. UK: under £135, no duty, but you must be VAT-registered and collect 20% at checkout. EU: under €150, no duty, but IOSS-registered sellers collect VAT from order one. Canada: CAD $20 — nearly every order is taxed. Australia: AUD $1,000 for duty, but GST is collected by the seller from order one. Japan: ¥10,000 combined, then duty plus consumption tax on the full value.

Why this keeps confusing people

Dropshipping looks domestic from the seller's chair. You take an order, a supplier in Shenzhen or Istanbul ships it, and the customer gets a parcel. Customs sees a different story: a commercial shipment crossing a border, with a declared value, an importer, and a tax bill. The parcel's journey decides who pays, and the rulebook is different in every market. The de minimis value guide has the full threshold map; this page is about what those thresholds mean when the package is yours.

The United States: the $800 gift that built the industry

Section 321 lets parcels under $800 in goods value per person per day clear with no duty, no VAT — the US has none — and no paperwork beyond a Type 86 line on the manifest. That one rule is why dropshipping as a business model exists: the economics are clean, and the buyer never sees a customs charge. Two warnings keep it clean. The threshold tests goods value only, so $780 of goods plus $40 shipping is fine, while $810 of goods is fully dutiable — the entire CIF, not just the ten dollars over. And the reform debate is not theoretical: Congress has repeatedly proposed cutting the threshold to $200, and the 2026 reform page tracks where that stands. Model the US on the assumption the line may shrink.

The UK: £135 is not a tax-free line, it's a handoff

Britain made a different choice in 2021. Parcels under £135 pay no customs duty, but the seller is required to be UK VAT-registered and to collect 20% VAT at the point of sale. Read that again: under the threshold, the tax doesn't disappear — it moves from the border to your checkout. A £90 order from a non-registered seller is a compliance breach, not a free ride. Above £135 the picture flips again: duty applies on the CIF value at rates from the UK Global Tariff (the China-to-UK electronics corridor shows typical numbers), and the carrier collects VAT plus a handling fee at the door. The practical rule: if UK customers are a meaningful share of revenue, register for UK VAT or sell through a marketplace that collects it for you. The EU-to-UK VAT registration guide has the mechanics.

The EU: IOSS is the price of clean delivery

The EU scrapped its €22 VAT exemption in 2021, so VAT applies to every import from the first cent; the €150 figure only covers customs duty. A non-EU seller has two ways to handle it. Register for IOSS — one registration, one return, covering all 27 member states — charge the local VAT rate at checkout, and the parcel clears without the customer paying a cent extra. Or skip it, and every order arrives with a VAT bill plus the carrier's €10–20 clearance fee at the door, which is a refund-request machine. There's also a rule change coming: the EU has proposed cutting the €150 duty threshold to €50 for 2027, which would drag far more parcels into formal processing. The Germany and France corridor pages show the per-market rates.

Canada, Australia, Japan: the three ways to lose money politely

Canada is the least friendly dropship market in the developed world: a CAD $20 de minimis means nearly every parcel is dutiable and GST-assessed, and the buyer gets a collection bill at delivery. There is no low-value channel worth building there — the China-to-Canada page explains the mechanics. Australia splits the difference: AUD $1,000 for duty is genuinely generous, but since 2018 the 10% GST is collected by the seller on every low-value sale, so a $900 order is duty-free yet still carries GST you must remit if Australians are your customers. Japan bundles everything under a single ¥10,000 threshold tested on the freight-inclusive CIF: under it, nothing; over it, duty plus 10% consumption tax on the full amount — a ¥9,500 item with ¥600 shipping crosses the line. The China-to-Japan page has the numbers.

Three parcels that ended badly, and why

A dropshipper declared $5 on a $40 phone case to keep the parcel under the threshold. Customs has seen every variation of that invoice; the declared-value mismatch is the most common inspection trigger in parcel traffic, and the penalty on a repeat offense dwarfs any duty saved. Undervaluing is not clever, it's a predictable audit.

Another seller shipped to Canada for weeks before the first dispute arrived: the customer was asked for CAD $18 in duty and GST at delivery, refused, and opened a chargeback. In markets without seller-collected tax, the buyer pays at the door — and buyers don't like paying at the door. Budget for it or price around it; the DDP pricing calculator shows where the tax actually lands inside your price.

And a third treated the supplier's shipping label as their compliance. The courier's broker cleared the parcel, the HS code on the manifest was wrong, and the correction notice came back to the seller of record — because that's who customs holds responsible, not the courier. Keep the supplier's invoice and the product's HS code on file per order; the HS code guide shows how cheap that insurance is.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who pays customs duty on a dropshipped order?

Depends on the destination and the threshold. Under most de minimis lines, nobody — the parcel clears free. Above them, the buyer pays at delivery unless the seller is registered to collect the tax at checkout (UK VAT, EU IOSS, Australian GST). The planning habit that saves the most money: before entering a market, find out whether the tax is collected at your checkout or the customer's doorstep.

Do I need to register for VAT to dropship to the UK or EU?

UK: yes, if you sell to UK consumers under £135 — the registration is a legal requirement, not a scale decision. EU: register for IOSS if you want VAT collected at checkout; without it your customers pay at delivery and your conversion suffers. The VAT registration guide covers the mechanics for both.

Is it legal to declare a lower value on the customs form?

No. Under-declaring to dodge duty is misdeclaration, it is the most common inspection trigger in parcel traffic, and penalties scale quickly for repeat offenses. The duty on most consumer parcels is small; the risk is not.

Which country should a new dropshipper target first?

For pure duty simplicity, the US — $800 threshold, no VAT, no registration. For tax simplicity with registration, the UK and EU once you've handled VAT/IOSS. Canada and Japan are the two markets where the threshold math works hardest against you; skip them until the volume justifies the compliance.
