Duty Stack Calculator

Four duties can land on the same shipment, and they all multiply the same number. A 2026 entry of Chinese furniture carries 8% MFN, 12.5% under the forced-labour action, and 25% if the line sits on List 3 — 45.5% before anyone mentions the metal tariffs. Most calculators stop at the MFN rate, which is why importers get brokerage invoices that do not match their spreadsheets. This one adds the layers in the right order, drops the ones that cancel, and shows the arithmetic line by line.

Four duties, one customs value, no compounding

The arithmetic that trips people up is simpler than the law behind it. Every ad valorem layer is assessed on the same customs value — the transaction value under 19 U.S.C. 1401a, which for US imports excludes international freight and insurance — and the layers are then added, not multiplied into each other. A product at 8% MFN with a 12.5% forced-labour duty and a 25% China list duty owes 45.5% of its value. Not 8% on the first pass, 12.5% on the total, then 25% on that: 8 + 12.5 + 25 = 45.5. On $40,000 that is $18,200 of duty, and the difference between the two methods is not small — compounding the same three rates would produce $21,975, a $3,775 overestimate that shows up when someone tries to reconcile the entry summary.

That is the whole reason this calculator exists. The tariff lookup answers what the HTS line says. The duty layers guide explains how the stack was built. Neither does the sum for a specific shipment with a specific list assignment, which is the number an importer needs before they agree to a price.

Where two layers cancel each other

There are two places the stack is not a simple addition, and both are worth knowing because they move the number in opposite directions.

The first is the forced-labour action. USTR's 2026 action assigns each economy a tier — additive 12.5%, additive 10%, fill-to 10%, or fill-to 12.5% — and the tiers behave differently. For a country in an additive tier, the duty is a flat addition to whatever the MFN rate is: Chinese furniture at 8% becomes 20.5%. For a country in a fill-to tier, the duty brings the total up to the tier percentage and no further, so a German part at 2.5% MFN carries 7.5% and lands exactly on 10%, while a Korean part at 0% carries the full 12.5%. Two origins, same nominal tier language, very different outcomes. The Section 301 guide walks the tiers country by country.

The second is the interaction with Section 232. When a 232 duty applies to a line, the forced-labour 301 layer drops out — the two do not stack, by the terms of the action itself. That is why the calculator greys that row out and reduces the total when you switch 232 on. What does not drop out is the metal duty: MFN still applies underneath it, AD/CVD still applies on top, and if the line is also on a China 301 list, that duty generally survives — most 232-covered lines sit outside the 301 lists, but not all of them, so the line is the authority and the calculator lets you add it manually.

What Section 232 actually multiplies

Until April 2026, the metals duty was assessed on the value of the metal in the product. A $40,000 machine containing $9,000 of steel was dutiable on $9,000, which is why the old 50% headline produced a bill of $4,500. The proclamation effective 6 April 2026 changed the base to the full customs value of the article. The same machine now pays 25% of $40,000 — $10,000. The headline rate fell by half and the bill more than doubled, and that single change is responsible for a large share of the invoice shock importers have reported since spring.

Two other details follow from the same change. The 15% metal-content test now decides whether a derivative is in scope at all, not how much of it is dutiable: above 15% by value it is a derivative and pays 25% on the whole article, at or below 15% it falls out of scope entirely and pays nothing under 232. And the exclusions are gone — the product-exclusion process stopped accepting and issuing requests on 10 February 2025, and the country arrangements were revoked on 12 March 2025 — so any advice that starts with "apply for an exclusion" or "import under the quota" is describing a regime that no longer exists. The 232 guide carries the tier table and the dates.

Where the calculator stops

Three things it deliberately does not do, because guessing them would be worse than asking you to look.

It uses a category-level MFN rate, not your line. The corridor database groups goods the way importers talk about them — apparel, electronics, furniture — and a category rate is a composite of the HTS lines inside it. A knitted cotton shirt at 19.7% and a polyester one at 32% both live under "apparel", and neither is 16.5%. Treat the output as the right order of magnitude and the right stacking logic, then replace the rate with your own from the HTS lookup.

It cannot know your AD/CVD case. Antidumping and countervailing duties are set per case and per producer, from 0% for a cooperating respondent to rates above 300% for a non-cooperating one, and they are frequently specific amounts per kilogram rather than a percentage. That is why the box is an input and not a lookup: the AD/CVD guide explains how to read an order.

It does not add the fees. The merchandise processing fee and the harbor maintenance fee are assessed on the same value but are not duties, and they have their own minimums and caps — the MPF and HMF calculator does that sum, and it is the difference between a duty estimate and a landed cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Section 301 stack with Section 232?

The forced-labour 301 layer does not: when a 232 duty applies, that layer drops out. The older Section 301 China lists are a separate question and mostly do not reach 232-covered lines, but "mostly" is doing real work in that sentence — check your HTS line against both lists rather than assuming either answer.

My goods are not from China. Why does the calculator still show a Section 301 layer?

Because the 2026 forced-labour action covers 60 economies, not one. The tiers run from additive 12.5% down to fill-to 10%, and the calculator applies whichever one your corridor's origin falls into. Goods from the EU or Japan can carry a duty under this action while paying a low or zero MFN rate.

Is the duty base the same for all four layers?

For US imports, yes — the transaction value, which excludes international freight and insurance when they are shown separately on the invoice. That is different from the EU and UK, where duty is assessed on a CIF value that includes freight and insurance. The CIF value calculator converts between the two bases if you are pricing for more than one market.

What about the $800 de minimis exemption?

It no longer exists. Section 321 was suspended for all countries on 29 August 2025 and made indefinite by CBP rule in June 2026, so there is no value below which a commercial shipment escapes duty. The threshold guide covers the remaining exceptions, which are narrow: bona fide gifts of $100 or less and personal articles accompanying a traveller.
